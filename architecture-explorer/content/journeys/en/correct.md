<!-- stage:inspect -->
## Did the document change, or was our interpretation wrong?

```diagram
The original has new content → a new observation
An earlier interpretation was wrong → a correction candidate
Distinguish the cases and inspect prior support
```

A source edit and a knowledge correction are different operations. Depending on what changed, preserve a new document state or review an accepted statement again. Inspect the earlier record so the correction identifies what it changes.

<!-- stage:version -->
## Why not simply edit the old content?

```diagram
Yesterday: review in progress → keep its evidence
Today: review stopped → record a new state
Earlier answers still identify their original support
```

Editing the old record erases the reason behind prior answers. Distinguish the new state while keeping earlier evidence identifiable. A source edit does not automatically correct every earlier knowledge judgment.

<!-- stage:review -->
## How is a new interpretation accepted?

```diagram
Earlier statement + new support
↓ Submit a proposed correction
Separate review → accept or reject
```

New content must not become accepted knowledge without review. Identify the requested change and its support. The existence of a review path does not prove every correction behavior has completed verification.

<!-- stage:history -->
## Should we keep when a statement applied?

```diagram
Earlier statement → when it applied
↓ Link its replacement or correction
Preserve history and distinguish the current view
```

Replacing a statement must not erase its support or temporal context. When a statement applies differs from when it was recorded. Temporal, correction and recovery findings remain open; this is not a promise of complete automatic repair.
